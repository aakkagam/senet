Senet: the ancient Egyptian race game

About Senet

Senet is one of the oldest board games known, played in Egypt for well over two thousand years. Boards appear in tomb paintings, in Tutankhamun's burial goods, and scratched into temple roofs. Two players race five tokens each along thirty squares laid out in three rows, moving by throws of four two-sided casting sticks. The path snakes through houses of fortune and hazard — the House of Second Life, the House of Beauty, the House of Waters — before the tokens bear off past the House of Horus.

This version follows the reconstruction published by the Oriental Institute of the University of Chicago: a playable, historically grounded ruleset with swaps, protected pairs, walls of three, and exact throws to leave the final squares.

How to play online

Two players share one screen — a phone passed between hands or a laptop set between you. Throw the sticks, then tap or drag a highlighted token: legal moves light up, swaps and rebirths play out on the board, and the special houses gate the final squares until you throw the exact number. No computer opponent, no account, nothing to install; the game runs entirely in your browser and remembers an unfinished match on your device.
